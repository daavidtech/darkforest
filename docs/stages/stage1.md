# Stage 1: Hunter-Gatherer

Stage 1 is the starting condition for every civilization. The civilization is a
small hunter-gatherer group with basic survival knowledge, local awareness,
simple materials, and no formal science.

The player is not managing a kingdom yet. They are managing a fragile group of
people trying to survive, grow, explore, and create the conditions for the first
major breakthroughs.

The main question of Stage 1 is:

```text
Can this group survive long enough to become a settled civilization?
```

## Technology Limits

Stage 1 has primitive technology:

- Fire
- Hand tools
- Basic shelter
- Food gathering
- Hunting and fishing
- Woodcutting
- Stone working
- Simple storage
- Oral knowledge
- Local scouting

Stage 1 does not have:

- Agriculture as an organized system
- Writing
- Formal schools
- Mathematics
- Metalworking
- Roads
- Currency
- Professional soldiers
- Long-distance communication
- Large-scale construction

Metal, fertile land, ruins, and unusual resources can exist nearby, but the
civilization does not automatically know how to exploit them.

## Starting State

The starter civilization has:

- One small camp
- Small total population
- Low carrying capacity
- Basic food stockpile
- Basic wood stockpile
- No metal stockpile
- No trained military
- No formal specialists
- Very low innovation score
- Local visibility around the camp
- Unknown map outside the immediate area

Example starter values:

```text
Population: 20-30
Happiness: 60-80
Innovation: 1-5
Food: 50-100
Wood: 80-150
Stone: 20-60
Iron: 0
```

## Resources

Stage 1 resources are local and physical:

- Food
- Wood
- Stone
- Hides
- Fibers
- Clay

Food security is the first pressure. A group with weak food income cannot grow,
support scouts, or afford many warriors.

Wood and stone support shelter, storage, tools, and basic defenses. Hides,
fibers, and clay can become prerequisites for clothing, ropes, pottery, and
preservation discoveries.

## Buildings

Stage 1 buildings are small survival structures:

- Central camp
- Campfire
- Shelter
- Food gathering site
- Hunting camp
- Woodcutting site
- Stone working site
- Storage pit
- Simple workshop
- Scout camp
- Defensive fence
- Ritual or teaching place

Buildings do not only produce resources. They create conditions for discovery.
Repeated food gathering can lead toward agriculture. Storage pits can lead
toward pottery or preservation. A ritual or teaching place can lead toward
writing, organization, or early schools.

## Population Roles

The player assigns people, not abstract labor points.

Stage 1 roles:

- Gatherer
- Hunter
- Builder
- Scout
- Primitive warrior
- Storyteller or early teacher

Population assignment is the core early tradeoff. More warriors improve safety
and raiding potential, but they reduce gathering, construction, scouting, and
innovation. More scouts reveal opportunities, but they create risk and remove
people from food production.

## Innovation

Stage 1 innovation is weak but important. The civilization does not run formal
research. Discovery comes from practice, observation, population stability, and
surplus.

Innovation grows through:

- Enough food to avoid constant crisis
- Stable happiness
- Larger population
- Repeated use of buildings
- Exploration reports
- Teaching or ritual places
- Contact with unusual terrain, resources, ruins, or other settlements

Innovation is reduced by:

- Starvation
- Low happiness
- Too many people assigned to warriors
- Damaged buildings
- Lack of storage
- Constant raids or instability

## Map Visibility

Stage 1 visibility is short range and uncertain.

The camp observes only the nearby area. Scouts are the main way to learn about
the wider map. Reports from scouts become the player's knowledge, but that
knowledge can become outdated.

Stage 1 can know:

- Nearby terrain
- Nearby food sources
- Nearby wood and stone
- Visible animals
- Visible ruins or landmarks
- Obvious nearby settlements

Stage 1 cannot reliably know:

- Distant enemies
- Hidden settlements
- Ore usefulness
- Long-range movement
- Advanced emissions
- Underground activity
- The purpose of anomalies

## Military

Stage 1 military is primitive and expensive in human terms.

Available military options:

- Primitive warrior
- Small raiding group
- Defensive fence
- Watch post or lookout

Warriors are assigned population. If they die, population is lost. A player can
build an early army, but it slows growth and discovery because the same people
are not gathering, building, scouting, or teaching.

Stage 1 combat is local. Long campaigns are hard because food, travel, and
coordination are weak.

## Discovery Paths

Stage 1 exits through several possible discoveries. The path is not identical
for every civilization.

Common transition discoveries:

- Agriculture
- Pottery
- Better tools
- Animal domestication
- Mining
- Copper or bronze working
- Writing
- Organized leadership
- Basic medicine
- Defensive construction

Different environments push different paths:

- River camp: agriculture, clay, fishing, transport
- Forest camp: woodcraft, hunting, concealment
- Mountain camp: stone, mining, defensive terrain
- Grassland camp: animals, scouting, mobility
- Ruin-adjacent camp: unusual discoveries, risk, hidden clues

## Stage Exit

A civilization leaves Stage 1 when it has enough population, stability,
infrastructure, and discoveries to behave like an organized settlement rather
than a survival camp.

Typical exit conditions:

- Stable food production
- Increased carrying capacity
- Storage capacity
- Several permanent structures
- Basic scouts
- First specialist role
- First meaningful discovery path

The next stage is not simply stronger. It creates new problems: larger
population needs, more visible footprint, higher coordination costs, and more
strategic exposure.
